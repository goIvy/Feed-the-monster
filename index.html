From first speech to finals

How a season runs

No experience needed. Most of our competitors had never given a speech before their first practice.

  1. Late summer

    Open mic night

    Give a 60-second speech about anything. Everybody goes. Nobody's graded.

  2. Early fall

    Skills lab

    Weekly workshops on structure, delivery, research, and taking notes on an argument.

  3. Fall

    Novice tournaments

    Your first rounds against other first-years, with a coach debriefing every ballot.

  4. Winter

    Varsity circuit

    Travel tournaments, bigger fields, elimination rounds, and speaker awards.

  5. Spring

    Qualifiers & nationals

    Compete for a spot at the state and national championships.

Speak the language

The flow
Your notes on a debate round, written in columns so every argument can be tracked from speech to speech.
Cross-ex
Short for cross-examination. Your opponent asks, you answer. Three minutes where rounds are won or lost.
Extend
Carry an argument forward into your next speech so the judge still weighs it.
Drop
An argument your opponent never answered. Point it out and it counts as conceded.
Turn
Take your opponent's argument and show it actually proves your side.
Speaker points
The judge's score for how well you spoke, usually on a 25 to 30 scale. A 30 is a perfect round.
Grace period
A few seconds after time runs out to finish your sentence. Not a new paragraph.
Cutting
In interp, your edited version of a published script or book, trimmed to fit ten minutes.
Ballot
The judge's written decision and comments. Read every one of them.
